04 / FIELD NOTESBUILD YOUR INTUITION
FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES

Know why
every part exists.

Short, practical explanations and self-check questions. Start with the five physical stages; then connect the mathematics to what you can see in the model.

01 Why do we need a compressor?+

Fuel adds energy, but a pressure ratio is needed so the hot flow can expand through a turbine and nozzle. The compressor's rotors add energy to the air; stators turn velocity into static pressure and align it for the next rotor.

02 Is it the combustion that pushes the plane?+

Thrust is an external force from pressure and momentum exchange with the engine. Combustion supplies energy to the gas; the turbine extracts shaft power and the nozzle accelerates exhaust. A jet also has ram drag from the incoming air.

03 Rotor versus stator—what changes?+

A rotor is attached to the rotating spool and changes angular momentum, doing work on the fluid in a compressor. A stator remains fixed, redirects the swirl and often diffuses the air, increasing static pressure without adding shaft work.

04 What causes stall and surge?+

Compressor stall is separated airflow on blade passages. Surge is an instability of the whole compression system involving large oscillations and possibly global flow reversal. Blade incidence, pressure ratio, corrected flow and rotor speed all matter; this page does not simulate a compressor map.

05 How is a turbofan different?+

A high-bypass turbofan has a large fan and a bypass duct. The fan accelerates a much larger mass of air by a smaller speed increment, making it more propulsively efficient at subsonic cruise. This visualization intentionally represents a single-stream turbojet core.

06 Why can nozzles choke?+

For a converging nozzle, when the total-to-back-pressure ratio becomes sufficiently high, the exit reaches approximately Mach 1. Further lowering back pressure cannot raise the mass flow at fixed upstream total conditions through that throat; the pressure mismatch then contributes pressure thrust.
